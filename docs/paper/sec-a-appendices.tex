\section{Changes from the second edition}
The third edition supersedes four potentially misleading readings of the
9 October snapshot. The actor is retained as historical machinery rather
than the current baseline preview. Several defects listed as open are now
repaired, but residual determinization and state limitations remain. The
41-game rating climb is preserved as a preliminary observation rather than
used to claim continuing progress through the plateau. Finally, candidate
promotion is no longer described as universally requiring the old paired
PPO test: later protocols include a documented C5 exception, pooled offline
filters and direct live A/B decisions. C13 is the controlled positive result;
C14 is not yet a result.

\section{Reproduction and build}
Build from \code{docs/paper/} with \code{tectonic main.tex} (or
\code{latexmk -pdf main.tex}). The figures are TikZ and pgfplots, with no
external images. To reproduce an experiment, record at least:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item repository and simulator revisions, format, dependency versions,
    feature profile, temperatures, device and seeds;
  \item exact teams with their order and generated-spread flags, learner side,
    opponent policy and revision;
  \item frozen input snapshots, collection mode and terminal, unfinished and
    rejected counts;
  \item for search: worlds, engines, screening sizes, the usage month and
    cutoff, $\alpha$, $\tau_o$, iterations and the seed;
  \item the evaluation unit (game, seed or opponent team), the panel's
    independence from development, and the multiplicity plan.
\end{enumerate}
Use an isolated data root for experiments, because the ordinary root is live
learning state. The offline test suite checks the implemented contracts
(enumeration, masking, collection modes, likelihoods, terminal recording,
reloads, scout non-anticipation, promotion predicates). Passing it is not a
measurement of playing strength.

\subsection{Reproducing the third-edition audit}
The tracked aggregate snapshot is fixed; a document build never refreshes it.
Run \code{python3 collect_evidence.py --verify} in \code{docs/paper/} to
recompute offline exact tails, both C13 round scores, Elo transforms and
normal-approximation LLRs without private inputs. The snapshot records the
source commit, UTC cutoff, manifest checksums, first-stopping campaign
receipts, performance cohorts and C14's unresolved status.

With the private ledgers available, \code{--collect --output PATH} creates a
\emph{new} snapshot by calling the repository's read-only ledger parsers.
It filters completed events at the collection cutoff, deduplicates rooms,
joins assignments, and truncates C13 rounds at their saved stopping counts.
It publishes aggregates only. It performs no login, battle, fit, service
change or promotion. Exact historical recollection also requires the
underlying append-only logs and campaign receipts; the public aggregate alone
cannot re-audit every original room or missing assignment.

New empirical claims are traced to public campaign documents and the frozen
aggregate audit. The original bibliography remains for historical results;
new game-theoretic context is verified against primary papers on robust
responses and imperfect-information depth limits \citep{rnash2007,brown2018depth}.
