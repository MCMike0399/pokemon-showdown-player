\section{Software and compute: making experiments survive real play}
\label{sec:engineering}

The campaigns depended on software work that is distinct from strategic
strength. A faithful reconstruction, a successful model fit and a reliable
browser submitter each remove a failure mode; none alone demonstrates an
Elo gain. This section records those changes and their measured scope.

\subsection{Search traces, per-team data and live priority}
The actor's SQLite episode is no longer the only data path. Search decisions
retain the private request, choice, sampled-search trace, timing and
configuration in append-only per-run JSONL. Completed games are reconstructed
into labelled positions for value learning and grouped by exact team
fingerprint. Per-team stores separate games, live positions, self-play,
value candidates, screening labels, gate results and promoted pointers. This
prevents a new team's examples from silently being treated as the previous
team's distribution. Content checksums protect engine-readable model files;
a missing or invalid pointer falls back to the hand evaluator or probe screen.

The value service can ingest in a live-only role while producers, training
and promotion remain idle. Repeated new games therefore grow a dataset
without necessarily changing the deployed policy. The old PPO supervisor is
paused during the search campaigns. There is no claim that the current
player updates its strategy after every match.

CPU contention was more consequential than GPU utilization. At ordinary
background QoS, concurrent work raised median live inference from roughly
2.3 to 10 seconds. macOS background QoS restored live responsiveness but
starved the simulations. The implemented alternative registers offline
process groups and pauses them around live search with SIGSTOP/SIGCONT,
letting them use ordinary scheduling while the human opponent thinks. A
stale pause marker is recovered after a crashed player. Later compute-priority
coordination also accounts for shared workstation workloads; it does not
control unrelated user applications. Campaign resource overrides permitting
RAM and swap do not make CPU interference free.

\subsection{MPS is faster at fitting, not at JavaScript simulation}
A learned screen scores joint actions before the expensive probe and matrix
simulations. It uses the 146-position plus 70-action inputs and a two-hidden-
layer 128-unit MLP, trained on within-world standardized probe scores. The
objective imitates the existing ranking, so low fitting loss is primarily an
efficiency opportunity. Both game-grouped validation and a newest-30\%
chronological test constrain retained actions, final-choice survival and
simulation savings. Test failures reject the fixed prefilter rather than
retuning its size against the test.

The original screening-only gate required point-estimate non-inferiority
and at least 20\% measured simulation saving. It was not an error-controlled
non-inferiority test with a declared margin. C10's combined screen/endgame
protocol instead required superiority in wins for its fixed bundle, after
at least 15\% predicted savings. This is a different preregistered question,
not a retrospective claim that the earlier screening gate passed.
Training takes seconds; evaluation takes hours or days. The GPU's idle time
is therefore not evidence that more epochs would remove the strategy plateau.

\subsection{Mechanics and reconstruction repairs}
An official simulator is exact about the state supplied to it. It cannot
repair a tracker that supplies a different game. The following repairs
therefore belong to the software history, including failures first listed
in the second edition's appendix.
\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{Enumeration and targeting.} Opposing double-Mega worlds now
 enumerate Mega and non-Mega variants before applying the one-Mega limit.
 Harmful ally-targeted status moves such as Parting Shot are pruned alongside
 damaging ally moves. Struggle rebuilds an exhausted moveset rather than
 feeding an impossible ordinary move into the simulator.
 \item \textbf{Identity and slots.} Ally Switch swaps the tracked actives.
 Illusion's \code{|replace|} is accepted without an HP field, transfers
 observations to the revealed identity, and restores previously known facts
 about the disguise. A switched-in healthy disguise no longer retains the
 illusion user's faint flag. Regression positions reproduced an all-worlds
 failure that formerly fell back to the first legal action.
 \item \textbf{Counters and pseudo-moves.} Failed Protect resets the chain.
 Public \code{-mustrecharge}, its consumption, and authoritative private
 Recharge requests restore the volatile; the targetless pseudo-move retains
 its request slot rather than undergoing ordinary name-based targeting.
 \item \textbf{Species and beliefs.} Forme aliases repair Floette-Eternal's
 Mega prior and own-set lookup. Equal-priority move order constrains sampled
 Speed, subject to explicit ambiguity exclusions.
 \item \textbf{Trace and chance integrity.} Preview/single-choice decisions
 clear the preceding trace. CRN matches chance streams across our matrix
 rows. C12 completion caches the original serialized world, including sampled
 sleep/confusion counters; regenerating missing cells from a new root would
 resample those counters and compare different games.
\end{itemize}
Sleep/confusion reconstruction still calls \code{Math.random} on the ordinary
build path. C12 preserves the sampled counters inside its cached roots; this
does not make all ordinary reconstructions reproducible from the exposed
search seed. Opponent PP and some history-dependent state remain incomplete.
Those residual limits are retained in Appendix~\ref{app:defects}.

\subsection{Transport reliability changes the experiment actually played}
The answered-request cache originally omitted the room. Identical preview
or opening requests in a new game could look answered. The player stalled
until its fallback submitted the third-ranked preview. This affected 231 of
634 audited C4 games (36\%); they won 47.6\% versus 52.1\% for the others,
a descriptive association rather than a randomized bug-effect estimate.
Commit \code{4b2800f} scoped the key to the room. That fix shipped with C5,
which is why the later segment cannot assign the whole observed rise to
search alone.

Team A/B added another transport state: which exact teams had already been
imported into the browser session. Remembering every imported team avoids
re-importing from an incompatible teambuilder view on the next arm switch.
The per-game override is validated before use; unavailable teams or preview
assets fall back to the baseline and emit an explicit \code{ab_error}.
Such events must be audited as treatment-delivery failures rather than
quietly presented as successful treatment games.

A browser page then froze during C13. All page evaluations hung, so the
180-second stall check in the same asynchronous loop never ran. The fix is
an independent thread probing the page every 60 seconds; after 300 seconds
without response it exits with code 75 and launchd restarts the player.
An account lock and active-room resume prevent a second controller from
starting another account session. This bounds a class of hung-page failures;
it does not recover every server disconnect or guarantee no timer loss.

The final team-setting handover also exposed a configuration-editing trap:
array replacement in a plist inserted an extra argument, causing a brief
startup failure. The corrected argument array was reloaded. Operational
handover, browser caching and model selection are distinct layers; each can
invalidate an otherwise correct strategic comparison if not logged.

\subsection{Pinned deployment and durable stopping decisions}
The player runs from a pinned live worktree, and each offline campaign uses
its own pinned source with frozen assets. Editing the development checkout
therefore does not mutate a game in progress or a running gate. Deployment
tests the proposed source, scans its public contents, selects the pinned
revision and requests a graceful restart at a game boundary. Model and
experiment changes are likewise adopted between games. Changing a launchd
plist requires unloading and bootstrapping it; merely restarting a loaded
job does not reload its configuration.

Persistent supervisors hash inputs, take locks, adopt existing worker PIDs
or resume original ledgers, and retry interrupted games on their original
seeds. Three exhausted attempts stop scheduling for inspection. An active
experiment cannot be overwritten by another campaign. A live H1 permits only
the exact options tested; merging multiple offline winners requires its own
combined live arm rather than assuming their effects add.

The gate scheduler once checked futility before acquiring a concurrency
slot. Every queued coroutine passed before any results arrived, so a
100-pair panel could decide at 40 yet run all 200 games. Checking again after
slot acquisition cut the serial reproduction to 80 games; concurrency four
can finish at most three additional games. The first stopping decision is
latched. Later rows are kept as \code{post_decision} and excluded from the
result, including after resume. This saves compute while preserving the
original scientific decision.
