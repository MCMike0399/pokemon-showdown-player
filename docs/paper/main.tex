\begin{abstract}
This report follows a complete autonomous player for the Champions VGC 2026
Regulation~M-C doubles ladder from an action-conditioned PPO actor to
determinized simultaneous-move search in the official simulator. A local
Apple~M5 runs the search, MPS model fitting, campaign evaluation, recording and
a Playwright transport; no language model chooses live turns. Learned
opponent and preview models use public replays, while private requests and
search traces support reproducible diagnosis. We retain the actor--critic,
GAE/PPO derivations and earlier evidence as an explicitly dated development
history, then extend the account through campaigns C1--C14.

The central result is the difference between better predictions, better
surrogate-game solutions and stronger ladder play. Sixty-one value candidates,
additional search compute, improved opponent predictions, scouting, a live
factorial experiment and a shared Bayesian root solver did not establish a
further live gain. C13 instead changed the team while holding the search and
preview fixed. Its randomized ladder comparison accepted the challenger after
897 games: 249/448 wins versus 210/449, an estimated +61.9 Elo on the
implemented score-difference scale, with a normal-approximation sequential
log-likelihood ratio of 3.054 above the 2.890 boundary. The subsequent
455-game champion cohort has performance rating $1344\pm33$ (descriptive
approximate 95\% interval), compared with the earlier rain plateau near
1200--1300. This is evidence for the team--pilot combination, not a learned
turn-policy breakthrough. C14 tests a different preview on that team and is
unresolved at the cutoff. The report specifies stopping rules, a documented
C5 promotion amendment, reconstruction and transport repairs, hardware
measurements, and the remaining statistical and information-set limitations.
\end{abstract}
