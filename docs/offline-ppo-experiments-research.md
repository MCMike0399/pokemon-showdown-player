# Offline PPO experiments on Macintosh

Research checked 2026-10-09. This document separates source evidence, repository behavior, and proposed experiments. The runnable notebook and its saved manifests should record the final implemented settings and measured results.

## What the Reddit discussion establishes

The post reports learning rate `1e-3`, eight environments, 60 decisions per environment between updates, and three epochs against random play. Rusenburn suggests `2.5e-4`, horizon 128, four epochs and four minibatches. Another comment suggests reducing or scheduling entropy regularization; the author proposes `1e-4` and subsequently reports slower, steadier learning, without confirming the final coefficient. Rusenburn recommends completed episode reward sums averaged over a deque of 100; the author says that method was already used. The author mentions TF-Agents. These are hypotheses and an anecdotal response, not controlled evidence. ## What this repository already provides

The policy is an action-conditioned actor-critic: it scores each complete legal joint action against observable state, masks padding, and obtains a categorical policy over the legal set. The `concat-v1` network encodes state and action separately; `matchup-v1` adds an explicit multiplicative interaction. The final action feature supplies a heuristic prior, so a new checkpoint is not automatically a uniformly random policy. A meaningful PPO learning claim needs an untrained checkpoint baseline with the same feature profile, prior and inference settings. [Policy implementation](../ml/model.py), [Feature encoding](../ml/features.py)

Before this experiment work, training used complete compatible episodes from one frozen revision, checked the original action likelihood, and rejected malformed trajectories. Rewards were zero before the terminal decision and equal to outcome (`-1`, `0`, `1`) there. GAE used gamma `0.99` and lambda `0.95`; advantages were normalized. Adam defaulted to `3e-4`, entropy weight was `0.01`, clipping was `0.2`, value weight `0.5`, gradient norm limit `0.5`, minibatch size 32, and whole-batch approximate KL could stop later epochs at `0.03`. These observations describe the inspected starting code; refer to saved experiment configuration for configurable values after implementation. [Trainer](../ml/model.py), [Trajectory checks](../ml/data_quality.py)

The offline simulator runs local Showdown, records actual learner requests and accepted decisions, and leaves truncated or erroneous games without terminal reward. The existing continuous worker uses paired seeds, sides and teams to evaluate candidates before deployment. The offline experiments should write into an isolated gitignored root and never promote into the configured live account's checkpoint. [Simulator](../ml/simulator.py), [Continuous learning workflow](continuous-learning.md)

## Proposed experiment matrix

The following is an engineering adaptation of the discussion, using this repository's terminal reward and complete games. `target_steps` means a minimum number of fresh decisions across completed episodes, with an overshoot recorded. It does **not** mean a fixed per-environment horizon. Literal horizon reproduction requires a separate vector rollout collector with correct nonterminal value bootstrapping; cutting complete-game trajectories and setting their terminal value to zero would change the algorithm. Official PPO documentation defines per-environment rollout horizon and total buffer size separately. [SB3 PPO parameter definitions](https://stable-baselines3.readthedocs.io/en/master/modules/ppo.html)

| Experiment | Learning rate | Entropy coefficient | Target steps | Epoch limit | Minibatch plan |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| High-rate reference | `1e-3` | `1e-2` | 480 | 3 | 32 samples |
| Entropy ablation | `1e-3` | `1e-4` | 480 | 3 | 32 samples |
| Lower-rate ablation | `2.5e-4` | `1e-2` | 480 | 3 | 32 samples |
| Larger-collection alternative | `2.5e-4` | `1e-2` | 1,024 | 4 | Approximately four minibatches |
| Combined alternative | `2.5e-4` | `1e-4` | 1,024 | 4 | Approximately four minibatches |
| Optional entropy schedule | `2.5e-4` | `1e-2` → `1e-4` | 1,024 | 4 | Approximately four minibatches |

The reference entropy and 32-sample minibatch are repository choices; the schedule is a new controlled variation. For a completed collection of `N` samples, `ceil(N / 4)` gives approximately four optimizer minibatches, and is not a fixed 256 when collection overshoots. Keep gamma, lambda, clip, feature profile, reward and KL gate fixed unless testing them explicitly. Record the actual number of optimizer steps and completed epochs because early stopping can reduce the requested budget. [Existing trainer](../ml/model.py)

Use random opposition for the primary matrix. Evaluate frozen checkpoints separately against random, tactical and heuristic opponents, plus an optional frozen self-play checkpoint. Alternate learner side, rotate a validated exact-format team pool, and pin simulator seeds. Keep evaluation seeds distinct from training seeds. Compare checkpoints from identical initial parameters for each training seed. At least three independent training seeds are useful for an exploratory run; five is a better follow-up once throughput is measured. These sample counts are experimental design proposals, not guarantees of statistical power. [Repository candidate evaluation contract](continuous-learning.md)

Plot completed episode return, its rolling mean, battle length and win/draw/loss counts against both accepted training decisions and elapsed time. Display raw variation and completed sample counts alongside smoothing. For this terminal reward, average return is `(wins - losses) / completed_games`; it is related to win rate but includes draws explicitly. Aborted games should have a separate count and must not be counted as losses. Plot held-out evaluation outcomes independently of training returns. Report a binomial confidence interval for win rate, and retain per-seed results so an attractive pooled curve cannot conceal failed seeds. [Outcome validation](../ml/data_quality.py), [Reward calculation](../ml/model.py)

Log policy entropy, policy/value losses, approximate KL, clip fraction, value explained variance, collecting-likelihood error and actual optimizer steps when available. A smoother training return can come from reduced exploration without better generalization. Judge the entropy hypothesis by held-out performance and convergence speed as well as variance. PPO alternates fresh interaction data with multiple optimization epochs; old human games do not supply the current collecting policy probabilities. [Original PPO paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347)

## Hardware experiment, separate from learning experiment

Benchmark actual simulator games per minute for candidate CPU worker counts, keeping each child at one PyTorch/BLAS thread initially. CPU simulator and protocol parsing work remain CPU work even when optimization uses MPS. Benchmark real variable-action training batches on CPU and MPS, with warm-up and GPU synchronization around timing. PyTorch's synchronization waits for all queued MPS kernels; omitting it can time dispatch instead of work. [MPS synchronization](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.14/generated/torch.mps.synchronize.html)

GPU support is checked with `torch.backends.mps.is_available()` and model/tensors must be placed on `mps`. MPS availability alone does not show a benefit for this small policy. SB3 specifically notes that PPO with an MLP commonly favors CPU and recommends subprocess environments for CPU utilization. Select a backend by complete throughput measurements, including transfer, validation, optimization and saving. [PyTorch MPS backend](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.14/notes/mps.html), [SB3 execution notes](https://stable-baselines3.readthedocs.io/en/master/modules/ppo.html)

An explicitly selected intensive profile can raise this experiment's worker count and training duty to use Macintosh fully while preserving a nonzero memory reserve and the repository's checks for shared services. Avoid many CPU threads in every simulator worker: more runnable threads need not yield more games. Choose the fastest measured worker count rather than the largest count. Persist the hardware inventory, software versions, selected profile and benchmark table with the run. [Existing resource policy](continuous-learning.md)

PyTorch provides a per-process MPS allocation fraction relative to Metal's recommended maximum working set. It caps MPS allocation rather than all host RAM. Keep a nonzero bounded fraction and independently inspect available unified memory. The documented high-watermark value zero disables the allocator limit and can cause system-wide failure. CPU fallback can be enabled for unsupported MPS operations; record backend changes so comparisons remain interpretable. [MPS process memory fraction](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.14/generated/torch.mps.set_per_process_memory_fraction.html), [MPS environment controls](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.14/mps_environment_variables.html)

## How the real-battle corpus contributes

Select files by verified format, provenance, schema and intended use, not just byte size. Preserve original source URLs, publisher license, regulation, file size and checksums in the local transfer manifest. Old formats remain historical transfer evidence. A compact exact-format public-log subset and existing normalized metadata are more useful for a first notebook than copying every compressed and uncompressed duplicate. Corpus inventory and transfer selection must be verified on DiveMac before describing what was copied. [Corpus policy](continuous-learning-research.md)

Use public replays immediately for battle statistics, observable pre-turn executed-move labels and an opponent scout, with whole-battle train/validation splits and conservative rejection of ambiguous events. Public logs lack the original private legal requests and human collecting probabilities. They cannot be passed to on-policy PPO as if they were this policy's sampled rollouts. Human imitation requires a separately validated observation/action adapter; imported scout data and newly sampled simulator PPO trajectories should remain identifiable in notebook tables and reports. [Existing replay research and leakage rules](continuous-learning-research.md), [Showdown protocol](https://github.com/smogon/pokemon-showdown/blob/master/sim/SIM-PROTOCOL.md)

Keep the initial PPO matrix independent of replay-derived auxiliary weights. Once it runs, test an additional pair of matched-seed conditions with and without an isolated scout or validated imitation initialization. This tests whether the copied real battles help the chosen benchmark rather than confounding every PPO hyperparameter comparison with an unmeasured corpus effect. [Repository separation of actor and scout training](continuous-learning.md)
