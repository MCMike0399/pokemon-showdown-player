\section{Introduction and scope}
\label{sec:scope}

Can one laptop play a current VGC format without a human choosing its turns,
learn from the resulting experience, and measure whether a proposed change
actually helps? The repository implements the full chain: connection and
legal choices, an actor--critic and its training records, official-simulator
search, public-replay models, a browser transport, and persistent experiments
on both local opponents and the live ladder. Its research history is also a
history of changing what counted as evidence.

The first edition (7 October) described MCP-supervised actor play. The second
(9 October) documented the move to browser-driven search, its promising
45-game paired comparison and its first 41 live games. The third edition
extends the record through 31 October and fourteen numbered campaigns. It
reports a long plateau, unsuccessful attempts to improve the search and
learned models, and a successful live team comparison. Crossing 1200 once was
already possible in the early trajectory; the new result is a controlled
improvement over the established rain pilot and a sustained post-promotion
performance estimate above that level. A transient peak and a repeatable gain
are different claims.

\subsection{The system at the cutoff}
Live turns use the C5 search configuration: six sampled hidden worlds, common
random numbers, up to six additional worlds on close decisions, a
win-probability-shaped leaf, selective Protect continuation and speed
inference. The baseline preview is the opponent-aware C4 matchup-value model
\code{pvalue-661448b6a320fce0}. C13 selected
\code{SunRain-Mev-Brisbane} over the long-used Recife rain team. C14 now
randomizes the preview between that baseline and a human-imitation argmax
model, with team and turn search held fixed. The old PPO machinery remains
implemented but its service is paused; it is not improving the live turn
policy after every game.

\subsection{Historical scope and kinds of evidence}
The implementation baseline for this edition is public commit
\code{bee08dc} (full hash in the evidence snapshot). The new live audit is frozen
at 16:28:15 UTC on 31 October 2026. It contains 7,734 completed, rated search
games, deduplicated by battle room, with 3,864 wins. This aggregate mixes
policies and experiments and is not an estimate of the current champion's
strength. Public aggregates, source hashes and numerical verification are
provided in \code{evidence-20261031.json} and \code{collect_evidence.py}
beside the paper. Raw requests, account identities and battle logs remain
private. No battles, training, live deployments or campaign changes were
performed for this article update.

Three kinds of statement remain separate throughout:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Implemented contracts}, traced to named source files.
  \item \textbf{Conditional mathematics}, proved under explicit assumptions;
    a certificate for a surrogate game is not a certificate for full VGC.
  \item \textbf{Measurements}, with their date, population, stopping rule and
    limits. Chronological deployment segments are descriptive; randomized
    ladder arms supply the stronger treatment comparison.
\end{enumerate}
Sections~\ref{sec:features}--\ref{sec:promotion} preserve the actor-era
architecture and proofs as the 7--9 October baseline, with scope notes rather
than silently reinterpreting old measurements as current ones.
Section~\ref{sec:evidence} likewise preserves the early evidence. New
campaigns, evaluation methods and software advances are in
Sections~\ref{sec:campaigns}--\ref{sec:engineering}. Appendix~\ref{app:defects}
updates the status of defects identified by the second edition.

\subsection{Format and pinned mechanics}
The target is \code{gen9championsvgc2026regmc}: doubles, bring six and pick
four, open team sheets when exchanged, one Mega Evolution per battle, no
Terastallization, Item and Species Clauses, and Champions stat points instead
of conventional EVs and IVs \citep{championsrules}. The simulator dependency
remains pinned to official Showdown commit
\code{c046106cbe075931b1ff8d8b800ff5be47a85f96} \citep{showdownsource}.
The \code{gen9} prefix alone does not specify these mechanics. Format,
simulator revision, exact team, model assets and effective configuration are
part of a reproducible experiment.
